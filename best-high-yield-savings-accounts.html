Best High-Yield Savings Accounts 2025

📅 Updated June 2025 · ⏱ 7 min read · 👤 Beginner-friendly

If your savings are sitting in a traditional bank account earning 0.01% interest, you're leaving hundreds of dollars on the table every year. High-yield savings accounts at online banks pay 40–50x more — with the same FDIC protection. Here's what's available in 2025.

What Is a High-Yield Savings Account?

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) works exactly like a regular savings account — you deposit money, it earns interest, and it's FDIC insured up to $250,000. The only difference is the interest rate. While traditional banks pay 0.01–0.5%, online HYSAs pay 4–5% APY.

The reason online banks pay more is simple: they have no physical branches, so their costs are lower. They pass those savings to you as higher interest rates.

What to Look For in Any HYSA

  • APY above 4% — anything below this in 2025 isn't competitive
  • No monthly fees — fees eat your interest earnings
  • No minimum balance — so you can start with any amount
  • FDIC insured — protects your money up to $250,000
  • Easy transfers — should connect to your checking account within 1–3 days

How to Open One in 10 Minutes

  1. Choose an account from the list above
  2. Go to their website and click "Open Account"
  3. Enter your personal information and Social Security Number
  4. Link your existing checking account for transfers
  5. Make your first deposit — even $25 works
  6. Set up automatic monthly transfers from your checking account
💡 Don't Wait for the Perfect Time Every month your money sits in a 0.01% account is money lost. Opening a HYSA takes 10 minutes. If you have $3,000 sitting in a traditional savings account, switching to a 4.5% HYSA earns you an extra $135/year with zero extra effort. Do it today — not next month.

Is a HYSA Safe?

Yes — all accounts listed here are FDIC insured up to $250,000 per depositor. This is the same protection offered by traditional banks like Chase or Bank of America. Online banks are just as safe — they're regulated by the same federal agencies.

The only difference is that online banks don't have physical branches. If you prefer walking into a branch for help, an online bank may not be the right fit. But if you're comfortable managing money through an app or website, a HYSA is a straightforward upgrade.

¿Qué es una cuenta de ahorro de alto rendimiento?

Una cuenta de ahorro de alto rendimiento (HYSA por sus siglas en inglés) funciona exactamente como una cuenta de ahorro regular — depositas dinero, gana intereses y está asegurada. La única diferencia es la tasa de interés. Mientras los bancos tradicionales pagan 0.01–0.5%, las HYSAs en línea pagan 4–5% anual.

La razón por la que los bancos en línea pagan más es simple: no tienen sucursales físicas, por lo que sus costos son menores. Pasan esos ahorros a ti en forma de tasas de interés más altas.
