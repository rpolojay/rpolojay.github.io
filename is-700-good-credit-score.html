Is 700 a Good Credit Score? What It Really Means

📅 Updated June 2025 · ⏱ 6 min read · 👤 Beginner-friendly

Short answer: yes, 700 is a good credit score. It puts you in the "Good" range, qualifies you for most loans and credit cards, and gets you reasonable interest rates. But it's not great — and the difference between 700 and 750 is worth thousands of dollars. Here's everything you need to know.

Here's why pushing your score higher actually matters financially:

The difference between a 700 and 750 score on a mortgage alone is $36,000 over 30 years. That's a compelling reason to push higher.

How to Push From 700 to 750+

  1. Pay every bill on time, every month. Payment history is 35% of your score — the single biggest factor. One missed payment can drop you 50–100 points.
  2. Lower your credit utilisation below 10%. If you're at 30% utilisation, pay down balances. Getting below 10% on all cards is the fastest way to boost your score.
  3. Don't close old accounts. Length of credit history matters. Keep old cards open even if you don't use them.
  4. Limit new credit applications. Each application is a hard inquiry that temporarily drops your score. Space applications 6+ months apart.
  5. Check for errors on your credit report. 1 in 5 reports has errors. Disputing and removing them can raise your score quickly — see our guide on how to read your credit report.
💡 How Long to Get From 700 to 750? With consistent on-time payments and lowered utilisation, most people can move from 700 to 750 in 6–12 months. The exact timeline depends on what's currently holding your score back — check your credit report to find out.

Cómo subir de 700 a 750+

  1. Paga cada factura a tiempo, todos los meses. El historial de pagos es el 35% de tu puntaje. Un pago perdido puede bajarte 50–100 puntos.
  2. Baja tu utilización de crédito por debajo del 10%. Si estás al 30%, paga saldos. Bajar al 10% en todas las tarjetas es la forma más rápida de aumentar tu puntaje.
  3. No cierres cuentas antiguas. La antigüedad del historial crediticio importa. Mantén tarjetas antiguas abiertas aunque no las uses.
  4. Limita las solicitudes de nuevo crédito. Cada solicitud es una consulta que baja temporalmente tu puntaje. Espacia las solicitudes más de 6 meses.
  5. Revisa errores en tu reporte de crédito. 1 de cada 5 reportes tiene errores. Disputarlos y eliminarlos puede subir tu puntaje rápidamente.
💡 ¿Cuánto tiempo para ir de 700 a 750? Con pagos consistentes a tiempo y utilización reducida, la mayoría de las personas puede moverse de 700 a 750 en 6–12 meses. El plazo exacto depende de qué está limitando tu puntaje actualmente.
