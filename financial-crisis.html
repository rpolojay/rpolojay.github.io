How to Get Out of a Financial Crisis

📅 Updated June 2025 · ⏱ 8 min read · 👤 Beginner-friendly

A financial crisis feels like everything is falling apart at once — job loss, debt piling up, bills you can't pay. If you're in that place right now, this guide is for you. Not vague advice — a concrete plan for what to do first, second, and third.

First — Take a Breath

Financial crises are incredibly stressful. The anxiety makes it hard to think clearly, which makes the situation worse. Before doing anything else, know this: financial situations — no matter how bad — can almost always be improved. People recover from bankruptcy, foreclosure, job loss, and mountains of debt. Your situation is survivable.

The goal right now isn't to fix everything. It's to stabilize — stop the bleeding, protect the essentials, and take one step at a time.

What Kind of Crisis Are You In?

Job loss or income drop

Immediate focus: cut all non-essential spending today, apply for unemployment benefits, start job search immediately.

Can't pay bills or debt

Immediate focus: prioritize essential bills (housing, utilities, food), call creditors to explain your situation, look for hardship programs.

Unexpected large expense

Immediate focus: negotiate payment plans, look for assistance programs, identify what can be sold or delayed.

Debt spiral getting worse

Immediate focus: stop using credit cards, list all debts, consider debt consolidation or credit counseling.

The Crisis Survival Plan — In Order

🚨 Do these in order — don't skip ahead
1

Secure housing and food first

Everything else can wait. Rent/mortgage and groceries are non-negotiable. If you're behind on rent, contact your landlord immediately — most prefer a payment plan over eviction.

2

Keep the utilities on

Electricity and water are essential. Call your utility companies — most have hardship programs that defer payments or reduce bills temporarily.

3

Cut every non-essential expense today

Cancel subscriptions, pause gym memberships, stop dining out completely. Not forever — just until you're stable. Free up every dollar possible.

4

Call your creditors before you miss payments

Credit card companies, loan servicers, and banks all have hardship programs — but they don't advertise them. Call and ask. Mention your situation. Ask about payment deferrals, reduced minimums, or interest rate reductions. Most say yes if you ask before defaulting.

5

Find emergency income fast

Sell anything non-essential immediately. List items on Facebook Marketplace today. Pick up any available work — delivery, temp work, odd jobs. In a crisis, $200 this week matters more than a perfect job next month.

6

Look for assistance programs

SNAP (food assistance), local food banks, utility assistance programs (LIHEAP), local nonprofit emergency funds, 211.org for local resources. Many people in crisis don't use these because of pride — use them. They exist for this exact situation.

What NOT to Do in a Financial Crisis

  • Don't ignore the problem. Unopened bills don't go away — the situation gets worse the longer you avoid it.
  • Don't take out payday loans. 300–400% APR turns a $500 problem into a $1,000 problem within months.
  • Don't cash out retirement accounts early. You'll pay income taxes plus a 10% penalty — losing 30–40% immediately. Last resort only.
  • Don't hide it from your household. Everyone in the home needs to know and be on the same page. Secrets make financial crises worse.
  • Don't make major financial decisions under stress. Selling your home or filing bankruptcy are sometimes necessary — but make sure you've explored all options first with a professional.

After the Crisis — Build Protection

Once you're stable, the most important thing you can do is make sure this doesn't happen again. That means building an emergency fund — even $1,000 prevents most financial crises from becoming catastrophic. See our guide on how to save $1,000 fast.

A financial crisis is also a powerful teacher. Most people who go through one come out with much better financial habits — more intentional spending, a real budget, and a commitment to building savings. Use it.

💡 You Are Not Alone Millions of people face financial crises every year — job loss, medical bills, divorce, unexpected expenses. It doesn't mean you're bad with money or that you've failed. It means something hard happened. The people who recover are the ones who face it directly, make a plan, and take one step at a time. You can do this.

Primero — Respira

Las crisis financieras son increíblemente estresantes. La ansiedad hace difícil pensar claramente, lo que empeora la situación. Antes de hacer cualquier cosa, sabe esto: las situaciones financieras — sin importar qué tan malas sean — casi siempre pueden mejorar. Las personas se recuperan de la bancarrota, la pérdida de empleo y montañas de deudas. Tu situación es superable.

El plan de supervivencia en crisis — en orden

🚨 Haz esto en orden — no te saltes pasos
1

Asegura vivienda y comida primero

Todo lo demás puede esperar. El arriendo y los alimentos son innegociables. Si estás atrasado en el arriendo, contacta a tu arrendador de inmediato — la mayoría prefiere un plan de pago al desalojo.

2

Mantén los servicios públicos

La electricidad y el agua son esenciales. Llama a tus proveedores de servicios — la mayoría tiene programas de dificultades que difieren los pagos o reducen las facturas temporalmente.

3

Elimina todo gasto no esencial hoy

Cancela suscripciones, pausa membresías de gimnasio, deja de comer afuera completamente. No para siempre — solo hasta que estés estable.

4

Llama a tus acreedores antes de fallar en pagos

Las compañías de tarjetas de crédito y bancos tienen programas de dificultades — pero no los anuncian. Llama y pregunta. La mayoría dice que sí si preguntas antes de entrar en mora.

5

Encuentra ingresos de emergencia rápido

Vende todo lo no esencial de inmediato. Publica artículos en Facebook Marketplace hoy. Consigue cualquier trabajo disponible — delivery, trabajos temporales. En una crisis, $200.000 esta semana importa más que el trabajo perfecto el próximo mes.

6

Busca programas de ayuda

Muchas personas en crisis no usan la ayuda disponible por orgullo — úsala. Existen exactamente para esta situación. Busca programas del gobierno, bancos de alimentos locales y organizaciones sin fines de lucro.

Lo que NO debes hacer en una crisis financiera

  • No ignores el problema. Las facturas sin abrir no desaparecen — la situación empeora cuanto más la evitas.
  • No tomes préstamos de día de pago. Las tasas del 300–400% convierten un problema de $500.000 en un problema de $1.000.000 en meses.
  • No lo ocultes a tu hogar. Todos en casa necesitan saber y estar en la misma página. Los secretos empeoran las crisis financieras.

Después de la crisis — construye protección

Una vez que estés estable, lo más importante que puedes hacer es asegurarte de que esto no vuelva a suceder. Eso significa construir un fondo de emergencia — incluso $500.000 previene que la mayoría de las crisis financieras se vuelvan catastróficas.

💡 No estás solo Millones de personas enfrentan crisis financieras cada año. No significa que seas malo con el dinero ni que hayas fallado. Significa que algo difícil sucedió. Las personas que se recuperan son las que lo enfrentan directamente, hacen un plan y dan un paso a la vez. Tú puedes hacer esto.
