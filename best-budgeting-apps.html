Best Budgeting Apps for Beginners 2025

📅 Updated June 2025 · ⏱ 7 min read · 👤 Beginner-friendly

The best budgeting app is the one you'll actually use. Some people want automatic tracking, others want manual control. Some need free, others will pay for the right tool. Here's an honest comparison of the best options in 2025.

Top Budgeting Apps in 2025

⭐ Best Free App
Mint Free
Automatic tracking Bank sync iOS & Android

Mint connects to your bank accounts and credit cards and automatically categorises your transactions. Great for people who want to see where their money goes without manually entering anything.

✅ Pros

  • Completely free
  • Automatic categorisation
  • Bill reminders
  • Credit score tracking

⚠️ Cons

  • Ads in the free version
  • Categorisation errors
  • Less control over budget setup
⭐ Best Paid App
YNAB (You Need a Budget) $14.99/mo or $99/yr
Zero-based budgeting Bank sync 34-day free trial

YNAB uses zero-based budgeting — every dollar gets assigned a job. It requires more engagement than Mint but produces dramatically better results. Users report saving an average of $600 in their first two months.

✅ Pros

  • Most effective method
  • Excellent education resources
  • Strong community support
  • No ads

⚠️ Cons

  • Monthly cost
  • Learning curve
  • Requires manual engagement

Personal Capital (Empower) Free
Investment tracking Net worth calculator Retirement planning

Best for people who want to track both their budget AND their investments in one place. Less focused on day-to-day budgeting, more on overall financial picture and net worth.

✅ Pros

  • Free and powerful
  • Investment dashboard

Envelope method Couples budgeting Manual entry

Digital version of the envelope budgeting method. Great for couples who want to share a budget. No bank sync — you manually allocate money to digital envelopes.

✅ Pros

  • Great for couples
  • Envelope method built in
  • Cross-device sync

⚠️ Cons

  • Manual entry required
  • Limited envelopes in free version

Which App Should You Choose?

  • Just starting out and want free: Mint — connects automatically and shows where your money goes
  • Serious about changing your finances: YNAB — the results justify the cost for most people
  • Prefer manual control: EveryDollar free version — forces you to be intentional
  • Track investments too: Personal Capital — best overall financial picture
  • Budgeting as a couple: Goodbudget — designed for shared budgets
💡 The App Doesn't Matter As Much As You Think The best budgeting app is the one you open every day. A free spreadsheet you check daily beats a premium app you ignore. Start with the free option, build the habit, and upgrade if you need more features. Consistency beats perfection every time.
