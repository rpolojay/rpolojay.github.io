What Is a Roth IRA and How Does It Work?

📅 Updated June 2025 · ⏱ 7 min read · 👤 Beginner-friendly

A Roth IRA is one of the most powerful wealth-building tools available to everyday Americans — yet most people either don't have one or don't fully understand why it's so valuable. Here's everything you need to know in plain English.

How a Roth IRA Works

With a Roth IRA, you contribute money you've already paid taxes on. That money then grows tax-free inside the account — dividends, capital gains, interest — all tax-free. When you withdraw in retirement, you pay zero taxes on any of it.

This is the opposite of a Traditional IRA or 401k, where you get a tax deduction now but pay taxes on withdrawals later. With a Roth, you pay taxes now and never again.

Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA

✅ Roth IRA

  • Contribute after-tax dollars
  • Tax-free growth
  • Tax-free withdrawals in retirement
  • No required minimum distributions
  • Can withdraw contributions anytime
  • Best when young or in low tax bracket

Traditional IRA

  • Contribute pre-tax dollars
  • Tax-deferred growth
  • Pay taxes on withdrawals
  • Required withdrawals at age 73
  • 10% penalty for early withdrawal
  • Best when in high tax bracket now

In a taxable account, those $428,000 in gains would be subject to capital gains taxes. In a Roth IRA, they're completely yours — no taxes ever.

How to Open a Roth IRA in 4 Steps

  1. Choose a provider — Fidelity, Vanguard, or Charles Schwab are the best options. All are free to open with no minimums.
  2. Open the account online — takes about 10 minutes. You'll need your Social Security number and bank account information.
  3. Fund the account — transfer money from your checking or savings account. Even $50 to start is fine.
  4. Choose your investments — for most beginners, a target-date fund or a total market index fund is the best choice. Low fees, automatic diversification.

Who Should Open a Roth IRA?

A Roth IRA is ideal if you:

  • Are early in your career and currently in a low tax bracket
  • Expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement
  • Want flexibility — you can withdraw your contributions (not earnings) at any time without penalty
  • Want to leave money to heirs tax-free

If you earn too much to contribute directly (over $146,000 single or $230,000 married in 2025), look into the "backdoor Roth IRA" — a legal strategy to contribute regardless of income.

💡 Open One Today — Even With $50 The single biggest Roth IRA mistake is waiting. Every year you delay costs you years of tax-free compounding that you can never get back. You don't need $7,000 to start — open one today with whatever you have and contribute more as you can. Time in the market beats timing the market every time.

Roth IRA vs 401k — Which First?

The recommended order is:

  1. Contribute to your 401k enough to get the full employer match
  2. Max out your Roth IRA ($7,000/year)
  3. Go back and contribute more to your 401k

The employer match on your 401k is an instant 50–100% return — always capture that first. Then the Roth IRA's tax-free growth makes it the second priority for most people.

Cómo funciona un Roth IRA

Con un Roth IRA, contribuyes dinero sobre el que ya pagaste impuestos. Ese dinero crece libre de impuestos dentro de la cuenta — dividendos, ganancias de capital, intereses — todo libre de impuestos. Cuando retiras en el retiro, no pagas ningún impuesto.

Cómo abrir un Roth IRA en 4 pasos

  1. Elige un proveedor — Fidelity, Vanguard o Charles Schwab son las mejores opciones. Todos son gratuitos para abrir sin mínimos.
  2. Abre la cuenta en línea — toma unos 10 minutos. Necesitarás tu número de Seguro Social e información bancaria.
  3. Financia la cuenta — transfiere dinero de tu cuenta bancaria. Incluso $50 para empezar está bien.
  4. Elige tus inversiones — para la mayoría de principiantes, un fondo de fecha objetivo o un fondo indexado del mercado total es la mejor opción.
💡 Abre uno hoy — incluso con $50 El mayor error del Roth IRA es esperar. Cada año que demoras te cuesta años de capitalización libre de impuestos que nunca podrás recuperar. No necesitas $7,000 para empezar — abre uno hoy con lo que tengas.
